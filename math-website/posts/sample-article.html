A Tiny Theorem

By You · Today

We prove a small but elegant identity. Consider the binomial theorem:

For $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$, $$ (x+y)^n = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} x^{n-k} y^k. $$

Setting $x=1$ and $y=1$ yields $$2^n = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}. \\tag{★}$$

Corollary

With $x=1$ and $y=-1$ we obtain $$ \sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^k \binom{n}{k} = 0 \quad (n\\ge 1). $$

Proof idea

Both facts follow immediately from substituting values into the binomial theorem and simplifying. Equation (★) also counts subsets of an $n$-element set.
